Case study — crypto exchange · licensed EU VASP

A crypto exchange rebuilt to read like financial infrastructure, not a token launch.

“Empresex is a crypto exchange that needed to read as financial infrastructure — not as a token launch.”

Yuliia Kruk, on the brief

Empresex is a licensed EU virtual-asset service provider — not a concept, a live exchange moving real accounts and real transactions. Nobody types a wallet address into something that looks like a landing page for a coin. The interface had to earn that trust before it asked for anything.

One rule ran the project: it has to look like it can be trusted with money within four seconds.

02The problem

This wasn't a taste problem. It was a trust problem with a number attached.

“Crypto interfaces default to neon gradients, glass cards and feature walls. That signals hype, and hype is the opposite of what someone feels before they move money.”

Yuliia Kruk

The one thing users actually came for — the exchange widget — wasn't the loudest thing on the screen. It wasn't on the screen at all, for a while. It was buried behind marketing.

03Direction

Two ways to design a crypto product. I ruled one out before drawing a single screen.

Considered, rejected

The neon-gradient, glass-card, feature-wall template

The default pattern for crypto products: saturated gradients, translucent glass cards, features stacked to signal momentum. It performs energy. Energy reads as hype, and hype is exactly the wrong feeling to give someone right before they move money — which made it disqualified on function, not on style.

Chosen

One system that reads like frames of one photograph

“One typeface (Helvetica), three type sizes per screen, and a single warm light source that crops differently on every page, so the whole product reads like frames of one photograph. Structure is drawn with 1px rules and negative space instead of boxes and shadows. Accent colour never exceeds 3% of any screen.”

Same brief, two very different products. Only one of them looks like something you'd hand your transaction history to.

07Where this case stops

What I'm not claiming here.

  • No conversion number The five-screens-to-widget count was measured going in. It hasn't been re-measured since launch — so there's no "after" figure here, because I haven't measured it, not because it wasn't worth measuring.
  • No revenue or growth figures Those sit outside design scope and were never mine to report.
  • No claim on brand reputation Public reviews of the business are mixed, as with most exchanges. This case is about the interface system that shipped — not an endorsement of the company.
  • No team to credit Built solo, so there's no "our process" here — only what one person actually did.
  • No claim past handoff Delivery covered the design system and the responsive front-end. Production deployment and ongoing engineering belong to the client's team.
